What this is

An interactive rendering of a four-level hierarchical partition of topographic change on the river Isar, measured between two lidar surveys flown in August and November 2024. It is the same partition the printed figures in the accompanying thesis are drawn from, held as a nested acyclic graph.

P0 is the voxel grid (0.25 m), shown as a 150,000-point sample of the 756,061 voxels for interactive frame rates. P1 – P3 are the three superpoint levels, each shown in full.

Superpoint colours for Class use the rebuilt label — the majority vote of each unit's voxels after reconstructing the class from Change_Code and is_transformed (dead band 0.25), as the thesis defines it in Section 3.6.4. It is not the raw Class5_Code column, which cannot be averaged safely.

Purity is the share of a unit's voxels holding its majority label, and the gate is the threshold applied before training: a unit passes at purity ≥ 0.80 and at least 10 voxels. 6,818 of the 10,061 first-level units pass.

The stack view offsets P1–P3 vertically above the point cloud and draws a line from every unit to its parent centroid — a literal picture of the hierarchy the contextual graph features are computed over. A slider then appears at the bottom to step through the levels one at a time, greying out the two not currently in focus without hiding them.
